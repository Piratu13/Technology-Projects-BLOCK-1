## Subtracting Binary Numbers
   - 1101 - 1010

   I subtracted the binary numbers from right to left. In the second column from the right, I needed to calculate 0 − 1, so I borrowed 1 from the third column. This changed the second column to 2 and reduced the third column from 1 to 0. I then completed the remaining subtraction. The final result was 0011 in binary, which is equal to 11.

   - 10110 - 1001

   I added a leading zero to the second binary number so both numbers had five digits. I then subtracted from right to left. In the rightmost column, I borrowed 1 from the column on its left, making the current column worth 2. Later, I borrowed again in the fourth column from the right because I needed to subtract 1 from 0. After adjusting the columns that lent a bit, I completed the subtraction. The final result was 01101₂, which is equivalent to 1101₂, or 13 in decimal.

   - 10001 - 1110

I added a leading zero to the second binary number to align both numbers to five digits. I then subtracted from right to left.

The rightmost column was 1 − 0 = 1, so no borrowing was needed. In the second column from the right, I needed to subtract 1 from 0. Because the columns immediately to the left contained zeros, I borrowed from the leftmost 1 and passed the borrow through the zero columns.

After adjusting all the affected columns, I completed the subtraction. The final result was 00011₂, which is equivalent to 11₂, or 3 in decimal.

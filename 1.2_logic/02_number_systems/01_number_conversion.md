## Conversion from One Base to Another
1. **Convert the decimal number 156 to:**
   - Binary
   
   To convert decimal 156 into binary, I repeatedly divided the number by 2 and recorded the remainder after each division. I continued dividing each quotient by 2 until the quotient became 0. Finally, I read the remainders from bottom to top to obtain the binary number 10011100.

   - Octal
   To convert decimal 156 into octal, I divided the number repeatedly by 8. I recorded the whole-number quotient and calculated the remainder from the fractional part of each division. 1. **Convert the binary number 101101 to:**
   - Decimal
   To convert binary 101101 into decimal, I assigned powers of 2 to each binary position, starting from 2⁰ on the right. I multiplied each bit by its place value and added the results. Only the positions containing 1 contributed to the total: 32 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 45.
<img width="920" height="540" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c57725df-8076-40b5-8ab2-91766461f35b" />

   - Octal
   To convert binary 101101 into octal, I divided the binary digits into groups of three, starting from the right. Each group, 101, represents decimal 5. Therefore, the two groups produce the octal digits 5 and 5, giving the final answer 55₈.
<img width="681" height="470" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b517ad71-f5e7-4fb5-83d8-6a705237846e" />

   - Hexadecimal
   To convert binary 101101 into hexadecimal, I added two leading zeros to make the number eight bits long. I then divided the bits into groups of four: 0010 and 1101. The first group represents 2, and the second represents 13, which is D in hexadecimal. Therefore, the final answer is 2D.

2. **Convert the octal number 745 to:**
   - Decimal
   To convert octal 745 into decimal, I assigned powers of 8 to each digit, starting from the right with 8⁰. I multiplied each digit by its place value and added the results: 7 × 64 + 4 × 8 + 5 × 1 = 485. Therefore, 745 in octal is equal to 485 in decimal.

   - Binary
   To convert octal 745 into binary, I converted each octal digit into a three-bit binary group. The digit 7 becomes 111, 4 becomes 100, and 5 becomes 101. I combined these groups in the same order to obtain 111100101.
<img width="696" height="473" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/e40eb780-4acf-4751-b768-25fd5389479b" />

   - Hexadecimal
   To convert octal 745 into hexadecimal, I first converted each octal digit into binary, obtaining 111100101. I added three leading zeros to make twelve bits, then divided the binary number into three groups of four: 0001, 1110 and 0101. These groups represent 1, E and 5 in hexadecimal, giving the final answer 1E5.
